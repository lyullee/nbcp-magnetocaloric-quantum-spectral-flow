Dear Editor,

Please consider our research article, “Finite-size, quantum-hardware accuracy, and package-shape gates for a sub-kelvin Na2BaCo(PO4)2 magnetic-refrigerator stage,” for publication in *Cryogenics*.

The paper addresses a practical gap between magnetic-refrigerant characterization and stage design. Public experiments already show that Na2BaCo(PO4)2 is a strong sub-kelvin refrigerant, but they do not specify a robust near-critical terminal field, field tolerance, or material inventory when its weak interactions remain uncertain. We combine public thermodynamic and diffraction data with long-range dipolar summation, constrained interlayer-model searches, and deterministic heat-profile stresses to convert that uncertainty into terminal-field, mass, duty, and measurement requirements.

The main methodological result is an adversarial model-risk audit. Starting from 631 exact local-grid and Sobol calculations, three adaptive rounds add 192 exact M4 evaluations near the public diffraction boundary and predicted engineering extrema. The audit retains 69 models and expands the compatible critical-field-shift range to 0.0334–0.2021 T, demonstrating that passive finite designs understated the decision range. Independent five-start calculations reproduce both extrema. Four lower-field fan points withheld from model retention add a genuinely unused test: holdout error rises with predicted field shift at Spearman rho=0.940. A second, orthogonal audit uses public 3.5 T inelastic-neutron data. An indicative 18.048 microelectronvolt bandwidth rule retains 30 of 69 models and reduces the sensitivity-envelope upper shift to 0.1476 T, but profiling the unresolved twin mixture leaves all 69 M4 models no worse than the source curve.

The central new result is a preregistered model-family size-transfer test with an engineering consequence. Four M4 anchors spanning the retained bulk-shift range are recalculated with M3 on N=18. Their optimum shifts span 0.293–0.425 T and peak-heat ratios span 0.537–0.852; 4 of four cross the declared major-transfer threshold. Common-grid, close-peak, low-state, and cutoff checks pass within N=18. This replaces the earlier one-extreme-model result and rejects universal N=12 transfer without presenting N=18 as a bulk solution.

We also provide a complete, decision-targeted quantum-hardware benchmark. Nine adaptive six-qubit circuits reconstruct all three low levels at all three spectral-flow nodes on IBM Kingston. Paired twirled-TREX and zero-noise-extrapolated jobs both recover the exact negative entropy-correction sign and separate it from zero. Zero-noise extrapolation reduces absolute bias by 25.8%, but the exact magnitude remains outside the reported-error interval. We therefore use the hardware result to establish a measured accuracy gate, not as a bulk material input or a quantum-advantage claim.

We convert that failure into a new measurement specification. Sixteen correlated N=18 stresses expand the design to 68 scenarios and give 34.55% unmeasured regret at 1.4890 T. Exhaustive search over 5,671 two-point panels selects 1.292 and 1.716 T, leaving 4.246% regret at a declared ±2.5% heat bound. A separate demagnetization calculation gives a conservative 0.136 T correction for a published plate geometry and requires a c-axis aspect ratio of at least 2.72 for a 0.02 T bound. Public second-material and device audits retain their independent validation roles.

The work fits the journal's coverage of millikelvin cooling, cryogenic refrigeration, low-temperature measurement, and cryogenic equipment. All experimental inputs are public, the calculation and verification paths are reproducible, and no new experimental claim is made.
